\newcommand{\PAPERABSTRACT}{
\noindent \singlespacing
We ask how the Affordable Care Act's Medicaid expansion changed where medical
residents are trained, in a twenty-year institution panel of the National
Resident Matching Program (2000--2019) that we construct, including newly
digitized archival match results. With ten pre-expansion periods and a null
pre-ACA placebo supporting the design, expansion is followed by reallocation
rather than contraction: intake at incumbent training institutions falls by an
imprecise $6$ percent that no conservative inference standard distinguishes
from zero, offered positions barely move, and training capacity grows through
newly entering institutions---many joining during the single-accreditation
transition---so state-level totals do not fall and, if anything, rise. This
decomposition reconciles hospital-level and state-level evidence that
previously pointed in opposite directions. An apparent role for state Medicaid
GME financing formulas does not survive reclassification to post-period formula
rules, leave-one-state-out checks, or Poisson estimation of the payment
response: the cross-formula contrast is a California phenomenon.

\vspace{1em}
