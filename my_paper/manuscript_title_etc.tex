\newcommand{\PAPERABSTRACT}{
\noindent \singlespacing
Does insurance expansion grow the physician training pipeline? In a
twenty-year institution panel of the National Resident Matching Program
(2000--2019) that we construct, including newly digitized archival match
results, we estimate how the ACA's Medicaid expansion changed residency
training, with ten pre-expansion periods and a null pre-ACA placebo supporting
the design. Training supply did not respond on any margin. Intake at incumbent
institutions falls by an imprecise $6$ percent no inference standard
distinguishes from zero; offered positions barely move; and, classifying every
post-2010 entrant against ACGME accreditation records, genuinely new
institutions show no differential entry---most of the apparent entry boom in
expansion states is the osteopathic single-accreditation transition becoming
visible in match data. State totals do not decline. An apparent role for state
GME financing formulas is a California phenomenon that survives neither
reclassification, leave-one-out, nor Poisson estimation.

\vspace{1em}
