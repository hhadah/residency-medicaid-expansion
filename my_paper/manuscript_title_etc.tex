\newcommand{\PAPERABSTRACT}{
\noindent \singlespacing
Does insurance expansion grow the physician training pipeline? We study how
the ACA's Medicaid expansion changed residency training using a twenty-year
institution panel of the National Resident Matching Program (2000--2019) that
we construct, including newly digitized archival match results. Every event
study displays ten pre-expansion periods, and a placebo estimated on pre-ACA
data comes back null. We find that training supply did not respond. Intake at
existing institutions falls by an imprecise $6$ percent that is statistically
indistinguishable from zero, and offered positions barely move. We classify
every post-2010 entrant against ACGME accreditation records and find that
genuinely new institutions show no differential entry; most of the apparent
entry in expansion states reflects the osteopathic single-accreditation
transition, which moved existing programs into match data. State totals do not
decline. A candidate role for state GME financing formulas does not survive
reclassification, leave-one-out checks, or Poisson estimation.

\vspace{1em}
